How to think about it

The price. Calling a bet B into a pot P risks B to win P + B, so it pays when your equity is above B / (P + 2B): 25% against a half-pot bet, 33% against a pot-size bet.

Your share. Equity is how often your hand wins (ties count as half) if the cards are dealt to the river, averaged over every hand the opponent could hold. Draws are worth roughly 4% per out on the flop with two cards to come and 2% per out on the turn. Made hands lose value against tight ranges, which hold more of the hands that beat them.

What this leaves out. The trainer treats the call as the last money in the pot. In a real hand, more betting can follow: implied odds make some draws better calls than the raw numbers say, and the risk of facing another bet on the turn makes weak made hands worse. The pot odds page covers implied odds.
